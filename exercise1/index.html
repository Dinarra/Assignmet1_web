Alan Turing

About the Father of Modern Computer Science

Alan Turing (1912–1954) was an English mathematician, computer scientist, and codebreaker. He is widely considered to be the father of theoretical computer science and artificial intelligence. During World War II, Turing played a pivotal role in cracking intercepted coded messages, enabling the Allies to defeat the Axis powers in many crucial engagements.

Lifespan: 1912 – 1954

Important Events in His Life

  1. 1912: Born in London, England.
  2. 1936: Published his landmark paper on the "Turing Machine".
  3. 1939: Joined the Government Code and Cypher School at Bletchley Park.
  4. 1945: Designed the Automatic Computing Engine (ACE).
  5. 1950: Proposed the famous "Turing Test" for Artificial Intelligence.

Key Contributions & Inventions

  • The Turing Machine (foundation of modern computation)
  • The Bombe (electromechanical machine used to decipher Enigma codes)
  • The Turing Test (benchmark for AI capability)
  • LU decomposition method for matrix calculations
